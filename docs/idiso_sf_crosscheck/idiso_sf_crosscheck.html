1What is being tested

Today electrons are selected with eleMVAIdWP95 (outside the ECAL crack) and Δβ relIso < 0.095. No electron SF is applied to MC.

The candidate is eleMVAIdWP90 && eleMVAIsoWP90. These are the heavy-ion forest's own classifiers: an XGBoost ID and an XGBoost isolation from HIElectronInfoProducer, each passing below a centrality-dependent cut. They are not EGM VID decisions, and the ntuple stores no EGM VID flag.

The closest EGM object is wp90iso. It is the Run-3 EGM MVA ID trained with PF isolation among its inputs, at the 90% signal working point. EGM provides one SF for ID and isolation together, measured with Z→ee tag-and-probe relative to a reconstructed electron, in cells of signed supercluster η × pT.

Because the two objects differ, so do their absolute efficiencies: our W MC gives εMC = 0.81 for the WP90 pair, while EGM's effMC is 0.85–0.95 in the relevant cells. So only data/MC ratios are compared. The test asks whether the data/MC ratio of our efficiency agrees with the EGM SF evaluated for our own electrons. If it does, the EGM SF can serve as the SF of our WP90 selection.

2Samples and the separate skim

The study runs in its own stream:

  • correction/idiso_sf_skim.C skims.
  • correction/idiso_sf_inputs.C builds the Combine inputs.
  • The fork's test/run_pO_idiso_sf.sh runs the fits.
  • correction/idiso_sf_plots.C draws the plots.

Every cut, bin and histogram name is defined once in correction/idiso_sf_common.h. skim/skim.C, the nominal Combine inputs and the nominal fit are not touched.

MC is the Aug-20 embedded production (POWHEG + Angantyr). Each MC event is filled with its generator weight, and each sample is scaled by ks = A·σ·L / Ngen with A = 16 and L = 46.5 nb−1 (skim/mc_norm.h). Every template is therefore absolute, with no area normalization. No lepton SFs and no LHE variations enter. Data are filled with weight 1.

3Event and electron selection

Per event, in this order (idiso_sf_skim.C):

  1. (a) Event filter: pprimaryVertexFilter (the analysis' PassEventSelection_pO) and |vz| < 15 cm.
  2. (b) Trigger: HLT_OxyL1SingleEG10_v1 fired. The Z tag-and-probe selection of section 7 is taken from these events.
  3. (c) Leading electron: the highest-pT electron with pT > 25 GeV, |ηSC| < 2.4 and outside the ECAL crack 1.4442 < |ηSC| < 1.566 (supercluster η). There is no ID and no isolation requirement at this step.
  4. (d) DY veto: the event is rejected if any opposite-sign pair of veto legs has 60 ≤ mee ≤ 120 GeV. A veto leg is any electron with pT ≥ 10, |ηSC| < 2.4, outside the crack, and relIso < 0.3, with no ID. Because the veto ignores the tested ID, it cannot bias pass against fail, and every Z tag-and-probe leg is also a veto leg, so no event enters both the W and the Z channels.
  5. (e) Trigger match: the leading electron lies within ΔR < 0.4 of an HLT_OxyL1SingleEG10 trigger object.
  6. (f) Isolation split of the leading electron:
    • relIso < 0.3: the total, the efficiency denominator. It is split into pass (eleMVAIdWP90 && eleMVAIsoWP90) and fail (everything else).
    • 0.3 ≤ relIso < 1.0: the QCD sideband, split at 0.6. It is kept twice: sball with any ID and sbid with eleMVAIdWP90 required.
    • relIso ≥ 1.0: dropped.

relIso is the analysis' Δβ-corrected PF isolation, (ch + max(0, neu + pho − 0.5 PU)) / pT (pOSkim::RelIsoPF).

For each category the skim fills three quantities:

  • PF MET, from all PF candidates;
  • mT = √(2 pT MET (1 − cos Δφ));
  • the lepton pT, only for mT > 40 GeV. This is the fitted variable.

Everything is split by the electron's charge and by the coarse bin of three schemes. The schemes use EGM's own cell edges, folded in ±η and merged in pT:

  • incl: one bin;
  • abseta: |ηSC| in [0, 0.8), [0.8, 1.4442), [1.566, 2.0), [2.0, 2.4];
  • pt: pT in [25, 35), [35, 50), [50, ∞) GeV.

Raw event counts, before the mT > 40 cut of the fit. In data only 4.3% of the total passes, against 80.5% in W MC. The data total is dominated by QCD fakes, which nearly always fail WP90 ID + iso.

Why the total carries relIso < 0.3

The QCD templates need an isolation sideband that is not itself part of the measurement. With relIso < 0.3 in the denominator, 0.3–1.0 is free for them. The cost is small: 99.29% of gen-matched prompt W electrons have relIso < 0.3, and 99.93% of passing electrons sit below 0.3 anyway. Our SF therefore differs from EGM's "relative to a reconstructed electron" definition only by SF(relIso < 0.3), which is assumed to be 1.

Why the pass-like sideband requires only the WP90 ID

An electron with relIso ≥ 0.3 essentially never passes the WP90 isolation MVA, so requiring it would empty the sideband. Requiring the ID half keeps the sideband's fake composition closer to that of the pass category.

4Efficiency and SF definitions

W sample

The population is the leading, trigger-matched electron of W candidates with mT > 40 GeV, the signal region of the nominal leppt_mt40 fit, in data and MC alike.

ε = NW(pass)NW(pass) + NW(fail)
(1)

In MC, εMC = Spass / (Spass + Sfail). Here S is the integral over the fitted bins of the W→eν signal template: the W⁺ and W⁻ samples, ks-weighted, in the reconstructed-charge channel.

In data the fail category is about 98% QCD, so the W yields cannot be counted. They come from the fit, N = r × S, with r the fitted W signal strength of that channel. Then:

SF = εdataεMC = rpassεMC rpass + (1 − εMC) rfail = 1εMC + (1 − εMC) rfail/rpass
(2)

Three consequences of eq. (2) matter for everything that follows:

  • The SF depends only on rfail/rpass.
  • rfail = 0 gives SF = 1/εMC, which is 1.24 inclusive and 1.31 at pT 25–35. This is the largest SF the fit can return.
  • rfail = 10, the upper edge of the allowed range, gives SF ≈ 0.41 inclusive with rpass ≈ 1.2. This is the smallest. The fail W is only 1 − εMC ≈ 19% of all W electrons, so near r = 1 the SF uncertainty is about (1 − εMC) × σ(rfail).

Charge-combined and scheme-combined values sum N and S over the included channels: εdata = Σc rpass,cSpass,c / Σc (rpass,cSpass,c + rfail,cSfail,c).

Z tag-and-probe

ε = 2 NPP2 NPP + NPF
(3)

NPP (NPF) is the DY yield in the both-pass (one-fails) category. A PP event holds two passing probes and a PF event one failing probe (section 7). In MC, εMC = 2SPP/(2SPP + SPF) from the DY→ee templates. In data, N = r × S from the Z fit.

5The EGM prediction for our electrons

The hypothesis under test is that, in every EGM cell c, our data/MC efficiency ratio equals SFEGM(c). Applying an SF means weighting every passing MC electron by the SF of its cell. The predicted data efficiency in a coarse bin is then Σi pi wi SF(ci) / Σi wi, where pi = 1 for a passing electron and wi is its MC weight. Dividing by εMC gives:

SFpred = Σpass wi SFEGM(ci)Σpass wi = ⟨SFEGM⟩passing MC electrons
(4)

This is the number our measured SF should equal if the pp SF describes our selection. The skim fills EGM-cell maps (signed ηSC × pT) of passing electrons to compute it:

  • Z: passing opposite-sign probes in DY MC.
  • W: passing electrons of the total category with mT > 40 in W⁺ and W⁻ MC, ks-weighted.

Its error is the per-cell total error averaged with the same weights. The average is linear, which assumes full correlation between cells and is conservative. EGM's crack edge is 1.444 and ours is 1.4442; the 0.0002-wide slice maps to EGM's [0.8, 1.444) cell.

6Measurement 1: the W sample with the in-fit ABCD

The idea, built the same way as the MB-based trigger efficiency (correction/trig_eff_mb.C), is to measure the efficiency on the W electrons themselves by fitting the W yield in pass and fail channels. The electron QCD is too large to count, so the W yields come from a fit whose QCD is modelled exactly as in the nominal leppt_mt40 fit (QCD_MODE=abcd). The QCD normalization is determined inside the likelihood, never assumed from a prefit number.

Channels and regions

Each channel is one charge (W⁺, W⁻) × category (pass, fail) × coarse bin k, named <C>_<cat>_k<k>. That gives 4 signal regions for incl, 16 for abseta and 12 for pt. Each signal region brings three one-bin counting control regions, and one Z channel pins the DY. The cards therefore have 17, 65 and 49 channels.

Prefit QCD inputs (idiso_sf_inputs.C)

  • B0, C0, D0 = data − Σ ks·EWK MC in each region, at r = 1. A value ≤ 0 would be floored to 10−3; this never happened.
  • A0 = B0C0/D0 is the prefit signal-region QCD.
  • The SR QCD template is the sideband pT spectrum at mT > 40 (data − EWK MC per bin, negative bins set to 0), normalized to A0 over the fitted bins.
  • The CR templates are one-bin histograms holding these counts. CRB also holds its own W, W→τν, DY and DY→ττ MC counts.

The likelihood

Why the fail W cannot be extracted

Four facts, each sufficient on its own:

  1. Size. The fail W is 1.4–1.8% of the fail QCD inclusively, and 0.4–2.7% across all bins and windows.
  2. Counting precision. The W⁺ fail control regions hold B = 31,604, C = 27,923 and D = 36,053 events. The Poisson error of A0 = B·C/D alone is √(1/B + 1/C + 1/D) = 1.0%, which is 240 events or 58% of the fail W. The residual lnN (15%) allows ±3,700 events. The counts cannot fix the W; only the pT shape can.
  3. Closure. The prefit (data − EWK)/A0 in the fail channels runs from 0.64 to 1.30 across bins and windows. The ABCD relation misses by thousands of events, 10–100 times the W.
  4. Shape. The sideband QCD pT spectrum is much softer than that of the fail signal region: (data − EWK)/QCD rises from 0.6 at 25 GeV to 2.5–3 above 80 GeV (figure 4). This is the known pT rise of the electron fake factor, measured at 4.4–4.8σ in qcd_abcd.C. The W template peaks at 35–45 GeV, where the ratio crosses 1, so the W yield and a QCD shape correction are degenerate.

Expected precision. In the Asimov fit (every parameter at prefit), σ(rfail) = 0.92 (W⁺) and 1.06 (W⁻) inclusively, against σ(rpass) = 0.039 and 0.043. Propagated through eq. (2), the expected SF error is ±0.135 inclusive, ±0.12 to ±0.22 per |η| bin, and ±0.17 to ±0.67 per pT bin. The pT scheme is worst because slicing in the fitted variable removes the shape information that separates W from QCD. Even with a perfect model, the W route measures the SF to about 13%, five times worse than the Z tag-and-probe.

On data the model does not describe the fail region. The W⁺ fail postfit has χ²/ndf = 46.4, with pulls up to 20σ per bin. The fit then uses rfail as a shape knob and drives it to whichever limit patches the mismatch best:

  • In the 0.3–1.0 and 0.3–0.6 windows it goes to rfail = 0 in most bins, giving SF = 1/εMC.
  • In the 0.6–1.0 window the template is softer still, and rfail = 10, giving SF ≈ 0.41.
  • Where rfail stays inside its range (5 of 24 per-bin results), it is 2–9 times the MC-predicted fail W.

Other symptoms of the same failure:

  • The same data give 0.75 (|η| bins combined) or 1.24 (inclusive, or pT bins combined), depending only on the binning.
  • In the inclusive fits the pass-channel QCD is pulled by θ = −1.0 to −3.2σ, with multipliers of 0.52–0.82.
  • The W channels drag the DY scale: rZ = 1.10–1.36 in eight fits and 2.27 in the |η|, 0.6–1.0 fit. There the fit predicts 419 ZPP events against 201 observed, while the Z channel alone gives rZPP = 1.085.

What does work: rpass = 1.14–1.20 inclusive is the same W normalization excess as in the nominal fit (effective r ≈ 1.21), and the pass postfit is good (W⁺ χ²/ndf = 1.04, p = 0.38).

7Measurement 2: the inclusive Z tag-and-probe

Selection (the same events, after steps (a) and (b))

Leg
pT > 25 GeV, |ηSC| < 2.4, outside the crack, relIso < 0.3. This is the W total's electron without the trigger match.
Tag
A leg that passes eleMVAIdWP90 && eleMVAIsoWP90 and is matched to an EG10 trigger object (ΔR < 0.4).
Pair
Among opposite-sign pairs of legs with 60 ≤ mee ≤ 120 GeV and at least one tag, the one closest to 91.1876 GeV. There is one pair per event.
Category
PP if both legs pass. PF if exactly one passes: that leg is the tag and the other is the failing probe. Two failing legs cannot occur, because a tag must pass.
Same sign
Pairs chosen the same way among same-sign legs. Used only as the background estimate of the cut-and-count cross-check.

Why a PP event counts twice. A leg is a probe when its partner is a tag. When both legs of a PP event are trigger-matched, both are tags and both are passing probes. In a PF event the passing leg must be the tag, which leaves one failing probe. A PP event with only one matched leg really holds a single probe. Counting it twice shifts ε by about 0.1% in data and 0.4% in MC, and the exact per-pair count moves the SF by 0.3% (0.9595 against 0.9564 in the cut-and-count).

The fit (its own likelihood, tag ztnp)

There are two channels in mee, with 60 bins of 1 GeV from 60 to 120:

νPP,j = rZPP(Z + Zτ)j + (W + Wτ)j + bPPNPPbkg/60,    same for PF with rZPF, bPF
(8)

Z is the opposite-sign DY→ee MC template of that category. The flat background has prefit size Nbkg = max(same-sign data count, 1), which is 1 for PP and 5 for PF, and its scale b floats in [0, 100]; r is in [0, 10]. W and W→τν are frozen at MC. The two channels share no parameter, so rZPP and rZPF are uncorrelated; the fit gives exactly 0.

Why a separate fit. It was first built inside the W likelihood, with one rZ shared with the DY of the W channels. The W channels pulled it: rZ ran from 1.10 to 1.21 and SFZ from 0.96 to 1.01 across W variants whose Z channels are identical.

8How every error is obtained

QuantityErrorHow it is computed
r, s, θ, bfit (Hesse)Symmetric errors and correlations from the covariance matrix of the fit result (fit_s of FitDiagnostics). All 10 fits have status 0 and covQual 3, a full accurate matrix.
εdata, SF (fitted)linear propagationσ²(ε) = Σa,b ga gb ρab σa σb over the r's involved, with ga = ∂ε/∂ra = +SaNfail/T² for a pass term and −SaNpass/T² for a fail term, where T = Npass + Nfail. Then σ(SF) = σ(ε)/εMC.
SF (expected)Asimov fitThe prefit prediction replaces the data (every r and s at 1, θ at 0). The Asimov fit is refitted and propagated the same way. This is the precision the measurement would have if the model described the data.
εMCnot includedMC statistics are neglected: the MC is about 100 times the data (0.52 M W⁺ MC events in the total alone).
SFEGM predictionweighted averagePer cell σ = (sfup − sfdown)/2 = √(stat² + syst²), averaged linearly with the passing-MC weights (full correlation).
pull vs EGM(SF − SFEGM)/√(σexp² + σEGM²)Uses the expected error, because the fitted error means nothing at a limit. For the Z the two are 0.0251 and 0.0259.
cut-and-countPoisson per categorySame-sign-subtracted counts, with the variance of each category = opposite-sign + same-sign counts. σ(ε) = √(4F²VPP + P²VPF)/(P + F)² with P = 2NPP and F = NPF.

A worked example: the Z error. With T = 507.4 probes, ∂ε/∂rPP · σ = (370.3 × 105.6 / T²) × 0.077 = 0.0116, and ∂ε/∂rPF · σ = (78.1 × 401.7 / T²) × 0.141 = 0.0172. In quadrature (the correlation is 0) this gives 0.0208, and dividing by εMC gives the SF error 0.0251. The 109 failing probes are the bottleneck. The relative errors are 7.1% on rZPP (1/√201) and 10.4% on rZPF (1/√109 plus the free background).

Why a fitted error at a limit means nothing. At rfail = 0 or 10 the minimum sits on the edge of the allowed range, where the likelihood is not a parabola; Hesse only reports the curvature of a function cut off by the bound. Examples: in the inclusive 0.3–1.0 fit, σ(rfail) = 0.014 fitted against 0.92 expected; in the pT 0.3–0.6 fit, bin 0 W⁺ has rfail = 0.027 ± 7.7.

Why the 5 interior W results have fitted errors (±0.03–0.05) 3–5 times smaller than expected (±0.12–0.26). They are evaluated at rfail = 2–9, a point where the model is wrong. There the fail W is a larger part of its channel, and ∂ε/∂rfail shrinks as T grows. The curvature at that point does not say how well the data determine ε; the expected error does.

What no error here includes:

  • MC statistics.
  • The QCD template shape. Only the window variation probes it.
  • The definition differences to EGM: W kinematics, the trigger match, relIso < 0.3 in the denominator.
  • For the Z: the flat-background shape, the mee window and the tag definition.

10How to read the plots

All SF plots are in correction/plots/idiso_sf_ele/ and are regenerated by ./run_idiso_sf.sh plots. The conventions shared by the SF plots:

  • Sideband window → marker: 0.3–1.0 black circle, 0.3–0.6 blue square, 0.6–1.0 red diamond.
  • Filled: every fail r involved is inside its range, and the bar is the fitted (Hesse) error.
  • Open: a fail r of the bin (either charge) sits at 0 or 10. The value is set by that limit and no error bar is drawn.
  • Grey box: ±expected error from the Asimov fit, centred on the plotted value.
  • Colour references: orange is the EGM prediction and green is the Z tag-and-probe result.

Prefit stacks of every W channel (window 0.3–1.0) and of ZPP/ZPF are in correction/plots/idiso_sf_ele/prefit/. The summary CSV idiso_sf_summary.csv holds every number drawn, with the boundary flag and the pull against EGM.

11What this does and does not establish

Established: inclusively, the data/MC ratio of our WP90 ID + iso efficiency agrees with the EGM wp90iso SF evaluated for the same electrons, 0.959 ± 0.025 against 0.956 ± 0.009. A deviation larger than about 5% (2σ) is excluded.

Not tested:

  • η and pT dependence. The Z sample (310 events) is used inclusively. EGM's per-cell SFs vary between 0.93 and 0.98, below what we could resolve, so the per-cell structure would be taken from EGM on trust.
  • Reconstruction efficiency. Our denominator starts from a reconstructed electron, as EGM's ID SF does. The EGM Reco SF (Reco20to75: 0.91–0.95 at 20–75 GeV, about −8% on electron MC) is a separate correction and is not covered here.
  • The relIso < 0.3 requirement in the denominator. Its SF is assumed to be 1; 99.3% of prompt W electrons pass it.
  • The trigger. It is measured separately in correction/trig_eff_mb.C: SF 0.9975 ± 0.0037 for mT > 40.
  • Any W-sample measurement with this QCD model. Making the W route work would need a fail-category QCD template whose pT spectrum matches the fakes in the signal region. That is the same fake-factor problem the nominal electron fit has.

Open: whether to switch the electron selection to WP90 ID + iso and apply the EGM SF per (ηSC, pT) cell. That decision is yours.

Questions you raised

Why the sideband is 0.3–1.0
The nominal uses 0.2–1.0, but here the total already contains everything below 0.3. The sideband must be disjoint from the measured sample.
MET or lepton pT?
The lepton pT at mT > 40. mT is an ABCD axis and cannot be fitted. MET is strongly correlated with mT.
Fit total and pass instead?
That cannot add information. Every pass event is also in the total, so the two are not independent samples, and fitting both as separate channels would count the pass events twice. Done correctly (independent pass and fail channels, or the total with a binomial split), it is the same likelihood as pass + fail. The fail-QCD problem would sit inside the total channel unchanged.
