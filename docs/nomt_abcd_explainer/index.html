The no-mT multijet method

How the new in-fit ABCD of the lepton-pT fit without an mT cut works, step by step, and how it differs from the in-fit ABCD of the lepton-pT fit with mT > 40. Every number on this page comes from the current inputs (22 October 2026, before the fit). Examples use W⁻ → eν in the lab rapidity bins, where the differences are largest.

1. Words used on this page

  • Multijet (also called QCD): events with no W, where a jet is reconstructed as a lepton (a "fake" lepton).
  • Iso-pass: the lepton passes the isolation requirement of the W selection. For electrons this is the WP90 MVA isolation and relIso < 0.3; for muons relIso < 0.15.
  • Anti-iso: the lepton is clearly not isolated, relIso between 0.3 and 1.0. This sample is almost pure multijet.
  • mT: the transverse mass of the lepton and the missing transverse momentum. W events peak near 60–80 GeV; multijet events sit mostly below 30 GeV.
  • Fake factor T (also written F): the number of iso-pass multijet events per anti-iso multijet event. It converts an anti-iso count into an iso-pass prediction.
  • Rapidity bin: the 12 bins y0 … y11 of the measurement. Groups: backward (y0, y1), central (y2–y9), forward (y10, y11).
  • Free scale: a fit parameter with no constraint (a Combine rateParam). Nuisance θ with κ: a constrained parameter; a log-normal (lnN) with κ = 1.10 lets a yield move by about ±10% at θ = ±1.

2. The problem: fake leptons

We count W → ℓν events in the iso-pass sample. Some of the events there are not W events at all: a jet was reconstructed as a lepton and happened to look isolated. The simulation does not describe these events well, so we measure them from data.

The tool is the anti-iso sample. It has the same selection except that the lepton fails isolation. It contains almost only multijet events, and it has many of them. If we knew how many iso-pass fakes there are per anti-iso fake, we could predict the multijet in the iso-pass sample:

multijet(iso-pass) = T × multijet(anti-iso)

Every ABCD method is a way to measure T somewhere it can be measured, and then use it where it cannot. The previous and the new method differ in where T is measured, how finely, and what the fit is allowed to change.

3. Why mT is the key variable

We cannot measure T directly in the signal region, because there the iso-pass sample is a mixture of W and multijet. We need a place where the iso-pass sample is almost only multijet. That place is low mT: a W decay gives mT near its mass, while a fake lepton with no real neutrino gives small mT.

So the plan of every ABCD method is the same: measure T at low mT, where the iso-pass sample is multijet, and assume the same T at high mT. That assumption, "T does not depend on mT", is the one thing both methods rely on. Section 9 shows how the new method tests it.

4. The previous method (lepton pT with mT > 40)

Here is the recipe, one piece at a time.

  1. Signal region. Iso-pass events with mT > 40, fitted in lepton pT. Everything below 40 GeV is thrown away.
  2. Three counting regions. B = iso-pass, mT < 30. D = anti-iso, mT < 30. C40 = anti-iso, mT > 40.
  3. The prediction. T is measured at low mT as B/D and applied to the anti-iso events at high mT:
    multijet(SR) = T × C40 = (B / D) × C40
  4. In the fit. B, C40 and D are three one-bin channels, each with a free scale (sB, sC, sD). The SR multijet is multiplied by sB · sC / sD. Because the W and DY in region B ride on the fitted r, the subtraction always uses the fitted W yield.
  5. How many sets. In the "abcd" model, one set of B, C40, D per flavour and charge. In the "abcdy" model (the last nominal), one set per flavour, charge and rapidity group, so three transfer factors per charge.
  6. The pT shape. The pT distribution of the anti-iso events with mT > 40, for all rapidities together. For electrons it is reweighted by a fake factor that rises with pT.
  7. Splitting into rapidity bins. The group total is shared among the bins of the group in proportion to each bin's excess of data over EWK at MET < 30 (the "low-MET excess").
  8. Residual uncertainty. One log-normal per flavour and charge: κ = 1.09 (μ), 1.05 (e). It covers the choice of the anti-iso window.

Worked example, previous method: e⁻, forward group, bin y11

Multijet counts of the forward group (y10 + y11): B = 157.9, C40 = 208.8, D = 241.9. So T = B/D = 0.653 and the group's SR multijet is 0.653 × 208.8 = 136.3 events. The electron fake-factor weighting raises it by 5.7%. The low-MET excess then gives bin y11 its share: 77.1 events of multijet, next to 80 W events and 173 data events in that bin.

5. Why we changed it

  • The mT cut uses the missing momentum. The electron missing momentum had a known data-only miscalibration (now corrected), and an mT cut moves events in and out of the measurement wherever the missing momentum is off. You decided to fit the lepton pT of all iso-pass events, with no mT cut.
  • Without an mT cut, the signal region contains region B. The iso-pass events below 30 GeV are now part of what we fit. Region B can no longer be a separate control region; it becomes part of the signal region. This is the structural change everything else follows from.
  • T varies more finely than a group. Within a group, T differs between neighbouring bins (Section 7.4). The previous method had one T per group (abcdy) or per charge (abcd) and shared the total by the low-MET excess.

6. New step 1: the signal region becomes two channels

Without an mT cut, the iso-pass sample of each rapidity bin is a mixture with two very different parts. Below 30 GeV it is mostly multijet; above 30 GeV it is mostly W. If we fitted them together as one pT histogram, the fit would have to separate multijet and W from the pT shape alone, which is weak.

So each rapidity bin is fitted as two channels: lo = iso-pass with mT < 30, and hi = iso-pass with mT ≥ 30. Both are lepton-pT histograms. Both use the same W signal strength r of that bin. Every event is in exactly one of them. mT now only decides which channel an event goes to; it never removes an event.

Summed over the 12 bins, for e⁻: the lo channel has 592 data events, 84% of them multijet; the hi channel has 2194 data events, 32% multijet. For muons the hi channel is only 7–9% multijet.

The key idea

The lo channel is region B, and it is now inside the likelihood as a full pT histogram. So the fit itself measures how much multijet there is, bin by bin, from the data that are 80–90% multijet. In the previous method, B was a single number per group outside the signal region.

7. New step 2: the multijet template of one bin

For each rapidity bin y and each channel (lo or hi) we need a prediction of the multijet pT distribution. It is a product of four factors:

multijet(y, channel, pT) = T_y × f(pT) × shape_group(channel, pT) × N(y, channel)

Read it from right to left: how many anti-iso events the bin has, how they are distributed in pT, how the conversion depends on pT, and the overall conversion level of the bin.

7.1 N: the bin's own anti-iso count

N(y, lo) is the number of anti-iso multijet events of bin y with mT < 30 (region D of that bin). N(y, hi) is the same with mT ≥ 30 (region H). For e⁻ y11: D = 92 data events (91.9 after removing 0.1 EWK), H = 101 (100.6). This factor is per bin, so each bin starts from its own amount of anti-iso multijet. It replaces the low-MET-excess sharing of the previous method.

7.2 shape: the group's anti-iso pT distribution

The pT shape comes from the anti-iso events of the whole rapidity group, separately for lo and hi. One bin alone has too few anti-iso events at high pT to give a usable shape. We checked that taking the group is safe: for every bin, its own anti-iso shape agrees with the group shape.

7.3 f(pT): the electron fake factor rises with pT

For electrons, T is not constant in pT: fakes at higher pT pass isolation more often. We model this as f(pT) = exp(s · (pT − 40 GeV)/10 GeV), so f = 1 at 40 GeV. For e⁻, s = +0.281 [+0.204, +0.357], which means T grows by a factor 1.32 per 10 GeV. A flat f is rejected (p = 0.0005). One slope serves all three groups (p = 0.60 for "same slope everywhere"). For muons f is flat, s = 0 (a fitted slope is consistent with zero: p = 0.10 for μ⁺, 0.62 for μ⁻).

In the fit, the slope can move: it is one constrained shape nuisance per charge, qcdFF_ele_Wp and qcdFF_ele_Wm, with its ±1 templates at twice the measured 68% interval. The prior is wider than the measurement because the lo-channel data that measured the slope are themselves in the fit.

7.4 Ty: the level of each bin

Ty is the fake factor of bin y at pT = 40 GeV, measured at mT < 30 from that bin's own iso-pass and anti-iso counts. It is measured with a profile likelihood that treats the small EWK contribution as a known background. This is the part that changed most: the previous method had one level per group.

Worked example, new method: e⁻, bin y11

Ty = 1.086 (68% interval 0.926–1.271). The lo template is Ty × f × forward-group lo shape × D, which sums to 77.4 events. The lo channel has 87 data events, 1.5 W and 6.4 other EWK: data/prediction = 1.02. The hi template is Ty × f × forward-group hi shape × H = 92.7 events, next to 81.5 W and 6.7 other EWK in a hi channel with 229 data events.

The previous method gave this bin 77.1 multijet events in mT > 40. The new hi channel starts at mT = 30, so it holds more events of every kind; the two numbers are not meant to be equal.

8. New step 3: what the fit is allowed to change

The templates above are the prefit prediction. In the fit, each rapidity bin of each flavour and charge has three free scales:

  • sT (qcd_sT_<F>_<C>_y<i>): the level of the bin. It multiplies the multijet of both channels of the bin.
  • sD: the anti-iso count below 30 GeV. It multiplies the lo multijet and the one-bin control channel CRD of the bin, which holds the observed count D.
  • sH: the same for H, multiplying the hi multijet and the control channel CRH.
lo multijet = template_lo × sT × sD hi multijet = template_hi × sT × sH × (window nuisance) × (transfer nuisance)

How the data pin these down, in order:

  1. CRD and CRH are counts of anti-iso data, so they fix sD and sH close to 1. They carry the statistical uncertainty of the anti-iso counts.
  2. The lo channel is 80–90% multijet, so its data fix sT × sD, and with sD known, sT: the level of the bin, measured inside the fit.
  3. The hi channel then gets its multijet from the same sT, its own sH, and the two nuisances of Section 9. What is left in the hi channel is W, which measures r.

The W in the lo channel is small (about 2% of the W events), and its pT shape differs from the multijet's, so the level sT and the signal strength r do not trade off against each other.

In the combined μ + e fit this is 12 bins × 3 scales × 2 charges × 2 flavours = 144 free multijet scales, and 96 one-bin control channels. The previous abcdy fit had 36 free multijet scales (3 per flavour, charge and group) and 36 control channels.

9. New step 4: the one assumption, and its two uncertainties

The level sT is measured in the lo channel (mT < 30) and used in the hi channel (mT ≥ 30). That is the assumption of Section 3: T does not depend on mT. The previous method made the same assumption (B/D measured below 30, applied above 40), and covered it only with its window uncertainty. The new method tests it directly and puts two log-normal nuisances on the hi multijet only.

9.1 The transfer: is T the same above 30 GeV?

Just above 30 GeV the iso-pass sample is still largely multijet, so we can measure T there and compare it with T below 30. We use mT 30–40 GeV for muons and 30–50 GeV for electrons. For electrons the W is only 14–37% of the iso-pass data even at 40–50 GeV, so the wider band doubles the multijet events and sits closer to where the hi multijet is (mean mT ≈ 48 GeV). For muons the W is already 50–57% at 40–50 GeV, so we stop at 40. The W simulation is subtracted at rW = 1.2, the level the muon hi data prefer; at rW = 1 the W excess in the data would be read as multijet.

The measured ratio becomes the κ of a log-normal: κ = 1 + √(deviation² + statistical error²). For e⁻: 1 + √(0.078² + 0.069²) = 1.104. For e⁺: 1.183 (ratio 1.167 ± 0.073). For μ⁺: 1.168 (0.983 ± 0.167). For μ⁻: 1.148 (0.995 ± 0.148).

Why one measurement for all bins, but a separate nuisance per group. Measured per group, the muon transfer is mostly statistical noise: it gave κ up to 1.98 for a ratio that is consistent with 1. The groups agree with the all-bins value (μ⁺ p = 0.62, μ⁻ 0.09, e⁺ 0.92, e⁻ 0.21), so we measure the size of the effect once, on all bins. But we still let each group move independently in the fit: there are three nuisances per flavour and charge (qcd_xfer_<F>_<C>_fwd/bwd/cen), each acting on the hi multijet of its group's bins, all with the same κ. A single nuisance per charge would act on exactly the same yields as the window nuisance below, and the two would merge into one.

One thing to watch: for e⁺ the all-bins ratio is 1.17 ± 0.07, more than 2σ above 1. The log-normal is centred on 1, so we expect the fit to pull this nuisance upward. The pull will show it.

9.2 The window: which events count as "anti-iso"?

The anti-iso window is relIso 0.3–1.0. If it were 0.3–0.6 or 0.6–1.0 instead, the predicted hi multijet would change slightly (with the lo channel fixed). This is a choice of method, the same for every bin, so it is one nuisance per flavour and charge that moves all bins together (qcd_rate_<F>_<C>): κ = 1.069 (μ⁺), 1.037 (μ⁻), 1.032 (e⁺), 1.042 (e⁻). It plays the role of the previous method's residual κ (1.09 μ, 1.05 e).

10. New step 5: the high-pT tail of the shape

Above about 50 GeV even a whole group has only a handful of anti-iso events per 2 GeV bin. Multiplied by the rising electron f(pT), single events became spikes in the forward groups, and in the backward groups the tail came out too empty because negative bins (after the small EWK subtraction) are set to zero. So above 50 GeV each group's shape is replaced by the shape of all 12 bins together, scaled to the group's own number of events above 50 GeV. The group's total and everything below 50 GeV are unchanged.

This roughly halves the template's statistical error at high pT (e⁺ forward, pT ≥ 60: 16.0 ± 4.6 → 16.0 ± 2.6 events) and fills the backward tails (e⁺ backward: 1.4 → 9.3 events). Above about 60 GeV the template still moves from bin to bin, because even all bins together have only a few anti-iso events there and the electron f(pT) is large. You can see this in the hi stack of Section 6. If it matters in the fit, the next step would be a smooth fitted tail.

12. What to look at after the fit

  • sT per bin close to 1: the lo channel agrees with the prefit level. A bin far from 1 points at that bin's Ty.
  • The transfer pulls qcd_xfer_*: e⁺ is expected to pull upward (its measured ratio is 1.17). A pull beyond ±1 in a group means the hi multijet of that group differs from the transfer we measured.
  • The window pulls qcd_rate_*, and the electron slope pulls qcdFF_ele_*.
  • The hi channel at pT > 60 GeV for electrons, where the template is thin (Section 10).
  • The electron W⁻ edge bins (y0, y11), where the previous fits had e/μ ≈ 0.7.
